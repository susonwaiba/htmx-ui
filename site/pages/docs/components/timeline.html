Markup

  • An <ol class="timeline"> of <li class="timeline-item">: a list in order, which is what screen readers announce. It is CSS only, with no behaviour.
  • Each item has a .timeline-marker and a .timeline-content. Inside the content, .timeline-time (a <time datetime>), .timeline-title and .timeline-description are all optional; any other markup works too.
  • The line to the next item is drawn for you, and stops at the last item.
  • Newest first or oldest first is up to you: the timeline shows items in the order they are written.

Markers

An empty .timeline-marker is a dot. .timeline-marker-primary, -success, -warning, -danger and -info colour it, and .timeline-marker-outline makes it hollow.

Put an icon, a number or an image in the marker and it becomes a circle; the line and the first line of text centre on it. Plain circles have a border; the colours fill them, and .timeline-marker-outline draws a coloured ring instead.

Progress and the current step

For a process (an order, a deployment, an application), colour the steps that are done, mark the current item with aria-current="step", which adds a ring and tells screen readers where it is, and leave the rest as outline markers. Numbers work as markers too.

Opposite content

A .timeline-opposite in every item goes on the other side of the line, right-aligned in a column --timeline-opposite wide (6rem): dates, times or amounts that read down one edge.

Alternating

.timeline-alternate puts the line in the middle and the items on either side of it in turn, with the .timeline-opposite, if any, across from each one. On narrow screens (below the sm breakpoint) it falls back to one side, so nothing gets squeezed.

Horizontal

.timeline-horizontal lays the items out in a row, each at least --timeline-min (10rem) wide, scrolling sideways when they don't fit. A .timeline-opposite goes above the line.
